Fantasy Range Projections — Hyperparameters

Model constants and hyperparameters

This is the honest inventory of the non-data values in the current prototype. Some are scoring/output conventions, some are numerical safeguards, and some are modeling assumptions. The modeling assumptions were selected as reasonable starting priors and checked through the Week 1 backtest; they were not estimated by an optimizer.

Exact formulas

Recency weighting

For target season T and historical season s:

raw_weight = 0.60 ^ (T - s)
weighted_rate = sum(raw_weight times observation) / sum(raw_weight)

The decay applies to every player-game row, not once per season. A season’s total contribution is therefore its games played multiplied by its per-game decay. For three 17-game seasons (2023–25), the normalized weights are 18.4%, 30.6%, and 51.0%. McCaffrey’s 2026 target-share calculation has a larger 2025 share because he played only four games in 2024: 2021: 3.1%, 2022: 12.4%, 2023: 19.4%, 2024: 8.1%, and 2025: 57.2%.

Shrinkage

effective_strength = strength times max(0, 1 - player_games / 48)
shrunken_rate = (player_games times player_rate + effective_strength times position_rate)
                / (player_games + effective_strength)

Strength is 14 for shares, 20 for efficiency, and 30 for TD rates. These act like pseudo-games, not literal games, and taper away for established players.

Expected team volume

There is no separate learned team-volume model. For each 2026 player:

expected_pass = mean(one row per destination-team game in 2025)
expected_rush = mean(one row per destination-team game in 2025)

QB attempts/carries are included. The code deduplicates to one season, week, team row before averaging. SF’s 2025 values are 33.76 pass attempts and 28.29 rush attempts across 17 games. Manual input overrides this baseline.

The simulation adds variance:

pass attempts = Poisson(Gamma(mean=expected_pass, CV=.18))
rush attempts = Poisson(Gamma(mean=expected_rush, CV=.20))

For a Gamma-Poisson variable with mean m and latent CV c, approximate variance is m plus (c times m) squared. SF’s pass-attempt standard deviation is about 8.41, not zero.

Role uncertainty

sample_penalty = max(0, 1 - min(games, 32) / 32)
instability = min(target_share_sd / .25, 1)

uncertainty = max(manual_override,
    .08 + .32 times sample_penalty + .22 times team_change
        + .22 times rookie + .12 times instability)

The .08 is a stable-veteran floor; 32 is roughly two full seasons; .32, .22, .22, and .12 are heuristic penalties; .25 is the target-share SD considered maximally unstable. None are fitted weights.

role_concentration = 70 times (1 - uncertainty) + 5

Lower concentration gives a wider beta-binomial target/carry share.

Calibration does and does not fix this

The output uses walk-forward conformal calibration: it adjusts final quantiles using completed historical Week 1 residuals. That improved 80% coverage from 74.9% raw to 83.0% in the backtest. It makes final intervals more honest, but does not prove internal values above are optimal.

Tuning order

  1. Replace targetable-pass fallback/clip and historical team volume with play-level opportunities plus dated totals/spreads.
  2. Fit role-uncertainty weights and beta concentration to future target/carry share variance.
  3. Tune recency decay and shrinkage strengths through nested walk-forward pinball loss and coverage.
  4. Fit yardage and TD residual distributions by position/opportunity tier.

The proper claim today is: transparent priors plus calibrated output intervals, not fully learned internal parameters.
